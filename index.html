THE SCIENCE, SIMPLIFIED

What is computer vision?

Computer vision is a type of AI that processes images. It learns patterns from examples and uses those patterns to describe or classify new pictures.

01
Pixels hold information

A digital image is made of tiny colored dots called pixels. Lighting, focus, and angle change what those pixels show.

02
AI recognizes patterns

A general vision model may describe shapes or colors, but it is not automatically trained or validated to diagnose dental disease.

03
Limits matter

Photos cannot reveal everything under a tooth surface. X-rays need specialized interpretation, and an AI can make mistakes.

RESPONSIBLE AI

Curious, not certain.

Being honest about uncertainty is part of good science. Here's what this prototype can and cannot do.

✓

What it can explore

  • Describe clearly visible features in a photo
  • Point out image-quality problems
  • Explain why a feature may deserve a professional check
  • Demonstrate how a vision model responds to an image
×

What it cannot promise

  • Diagnose cavities, gum disease, or infection
  • Prove that teeth are healthy
  • Replace a dental exam or X-ray interpretation
  • Give clinically validated confidence percentages
TAKE IT TO THE FAIR

Run a small experiment.

Compare AI descriptions with reliable labels from educational images. This helps you study consistency—not prove medical accuracy.

SCIENCE FAIR METHOD

Test. Record. Reflect.

Use a small set of appropriately licensed educational images with labels supplied by a reliable source. Decide your scoring rules before testing, and never use people's private images without permission.

  1. Choose and record the source of each image.
  2. Write down the trusted reference label before AI analysis.
  3. Record the model's observation exactly and compare fairly.
  4. Report mismatches and limitations, not just successful examples.
